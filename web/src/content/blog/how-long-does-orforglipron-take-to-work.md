If you just started orforglipron — or are about to — "how long does orforglipron take to work?" is really three different questions wearing one trench coat. Here's the honest, FDA-label-grounded breakdown of each timeline, without inventing a day count the label doesn't publish.

## So, how long does orforglipron actually take to work?

Short version: it depends which "work" you mean. Appetite-related effects build gradually as the dose increases and aren't tied to a published day count. Reaching the higher, optional doses takes a minimum of several months under the FDA label's 30-day-per-step titration schedule. And the trial-measured weight-loss percentages in Foundayo's label reflect a full 72-week study, not an early snapshot. The rest of this article walks through each of those three timelines in detail.

## Three timelines hiding inside one question

"Working" can mean:

1. **Early physiological effects** — appetite and fullness changes as a GLP-1 receptor agonist.
2. **Visible weight change** — the number on the scale moving.
3. **The trial-measured result** — the actual percentages in Foundayo's FDA label.

These don't happen on the same schedule, and mixing them up is why "how long does it take to work" doesn't have a one-line answer. The one thing that *is* published and exact is the titration schedule — so that's the most reliable place to start.

## Why the titration schedule sets the pace of everything

Per the FDA-approved FOUNDAYO label, orforglipron starts at 0.8 mg once daily and increases in steps, each held for **at least 30 days** before the next:

| Step | Dose | Earliest day it can start |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.8 mg | Day 1 |
| 2 | 2.5 mg | Day 31 |
| 3 | 5.5 mg | Day 61 |
| 4 (optional) | 9 mg | Day 91 |
| 5 (optional) | 14.5 mg | Day 121 |
| 6 (optional) | 17.2 mg (max) | Day 151 |

That "earliest possible" column is just arithmetic on the label's own 30-day minimum — real-world pacing is often slower, since each increase depends on your response and tolerability, not a fixed calendar. Our [step-by-step orforglipron guide](/blog/how-to-take-orforglipron/) covers the full schedule and administration details. The practical takeaway: even at the fastest label-allowed pace, reaching the maximum dose takes about five months. There's no version of "how long does it work" that skips this — the dose you're on shapes what you should reasonably expect to feel.

## When appetite changes show up vs. when the scale moves

The FDA label doesn't publish a specific week or day when appetite effects begin — that's a gap worth being upfront about rather than filling with a guessed number. What the label does establish is the mechanism: orforglipron is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, a class associated with slowed gastric emptying and increased satiety signaling, which is the basis for reduced appetite and intake. How quickly any one person notices that, and how strongly, isn't something the label or trials quantify at an individual level — it's reasonable to expect it to build gradually alongside the dose increases rather than switch on at once.

Visible weight change tends to lag a step behind appetite change, since it takes sustained lower intake over time to show up on a scale. Our [orforglipron weight loss results guide](/blog/orforglipron-weight-loss/) breaks down exactly what the trials measured, dose by dose — but that data was collected at the 72-week trial endpoint, not at an early checkpoint, which is the next thing worth being precise about.

## What the trials actually measured — and why there's no week-4 percentage

Foundayo's published efficacy numbers come from two phase 3 trials, both running 72 weeks (about 16-17 months), with the dose titrated upward throughout. The FDA label's Table 6 reports the group-average percent weight change **at week 72** — it does not publish an interim week-4 or week-12 percentage. That means there's no official early-checkpoint number to hold your own progress up against; the label's efficacy data is a long-run reference point, not a short-term prediction. If you want the actual week-72 figures by dose, they're covered in detail in our weight-loss results article linked above.

## How this compares to other GLP-1 timelines

Orforglipron isn't unusually slow to titrate — it's typical of the class. Per MedlinePlus, oral semaglutide (Rybelsus) starts at 3 mg once daily for at least 30 days — a dose its own label notes isn't effective for blood-sugar control on its own — before stepping up to 7 mg, and optionally 14 mg after another 30 days. Injectable semaglutide (Ozempic) is titrated on its own weekly schedule as well, per MedlinePlus.

| | Orforglipron (Foundayo) | Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) |
|---|---|---|
| Starting dose | 0.8 mg | 3 mg |
| Step-up interval | At least 30 days per step | At least 30 days per step |
| Number of steps to max labeled dose | Up to 6 (0.8→2.5→5.5→9→14.5→17.2 mg) | Up to 3 (3→7→14 mg) |
| Earliest calendar time to reach max dose | ~150 days (about 5 months) | ~60 days (about 2 months) |
| Approved use | Chronic weight management | Type 2 diabetes (blood-sugar control) |

The step counts and per-step minimums above come directly from each drug's own label pattern (Foundayo per DailyMed, Rybelsus per MedlinePlus); the "earliest calendar time" columns are simple arithmetic on those minimums, not a claim either label states outright. Every GLP-1 medication in this class builds up gradually rather than starting at a treatment dose, which is a deliberate tolerability measure, not a flaw specific to any one drug — and the two drugs are approved for different uses, so this is a titration-pace comparison, not an efficacy or "better" comparison. ## What to do if you don't feel anything is happening yet

There's no FDA-published "normal" number of weeks before someone should notice appetite changes, so there's nothing official to measure yourself against. If you're a few weeks in and not noticing much, that isn't automatically a problem — you may simply be on an early, lower dose where effects are still building. The right move is to bring it up with your prescriber at your next check-in; they can look at where you are in the titration schedule and decide whether anything should change. This article can't tell you whether your specific timeline is on track — that's a clinical judgment, not a content judgment.
