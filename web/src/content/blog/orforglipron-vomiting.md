Yes — vomiting is listed as a common gastrointestinal side effect on orforglipron's (Foundayo) FDA-approved label, in the same category as nausea, diarrhea, and constipation. It's usually less frequent than nausea, tends to cluster around starting treatment or a dose increase, and is often manageable with simple supportive steps — but persistent or severe vomiting is a reason to call your prescriber, not wait it out.

## Is vomiting a listed orforglipron side effect?

Yes. Per the FDA-approved FOUNDAYO (orforglipron) label on DailyMed, vomiting appears among the common adverse reactions reported in the drug's clinical trials, alongside nausea, diarrhea, and constipation. Nausea is reported more frequently than vomiting, but vomiting is still a recognized, labeled effect rather than a rare or unexpected one.

This is consistent with the wider GLP-1 receptor agonist class. Semaglutide, the active ingredient in Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus, is also associated with vomiting in its drug information, per MedlinePlus. There's no published trial directly comparing vomiting rates between orforglipron and semaglutide, so this article won't claim one causes more or less of it — that head-to-head comparison hasn't been run.

## Why does orforglipron cause vomiting?

Orforglipron is a **GLP-1 receptor agonist** — it works partly by slowing gastric emptying, meaning food stays in your stomach longer, and by acting on appetite and nausea signaling in the brain. That same mechanism, which is central to how the drug supports weight management, is also what commonly drives nausea and, for some people, vomiting — especially early in treatment or right after a dose increase. If you want the underlying mechanism explained in more depth, see [How Does Orforglipron Work?](/blog/how-does-orforglipron-work/)

Vomiting on orforglipron generally doesn't happen in isolation — it tends to show up alongside or after nausea, as an extension of the same slowed-digestion pattern rather than a separate, unrelated symptom. If nausea without vomiting is your main issue, [Managing Orforglipron Nausea](/blog/managing-orforglipron-nausea/) covers that side specifically.

## When is vomiting most likely?

Like the other common GI side effects, vomiting tends to track the dose titration schedule rather than showing up randomly throughout treatment.

| Timing | What's happening | Vomiting likelihood |
|---|---|---|
| First 1-2 weeks (starting dose) | Body adjusting to the drug | Possible, usually mild if it occurs |
| Around each dose increase | New dose level, slower gastric emptying | More likely to notice |
| Weeks after a stable dose | Body has adapted | Typically eases |

FOUNDAYO's FDA-approved titration schedule holds each dose for at least 30 days before increasing, which gives your body a stretch of time to adjust at each level before the next step. For the full schedule, see [How to Take Orforglipron](/blog/how-to-take-orforglipron/) or the broader adjustment window in [First Week on Orforglipron](/blog/first-week-on-orforglipron/).

## What helps manage vomiting on orforglipron

These are general, commonly discussed supportive steps for GLP-1-related GI side effects — not medical advice, and not a substitute for calling your prescriber if symptoms are frequent, severe, or don't improve:

- **Eat smaller, slower meals.** A stomach that's emptying more slowly has less tolerance for large volumes eaten quickly, which can trigger vomiting on top of nausea.
- **Go easy on greasy, fried, or heavily spiced food** while symptoms are active — rich foods digest slowly and tend to worsen both nausea and vomiting.
- **Sip fluids steadily through the day** rather than large amounts at once, especially important if you're losing fluids through vomiting.
- **Stop eating before you feel full.** Overfilling a slow-emptying stomach is a common trigger for both nausea and vomiting.
- **Don't rush the titration.** If a dose is proving rough, prescribers can hold you at that level longer before increasing rather than pushing through.

## When to contact your prescriber

Reach out promptly, rather than waiting it out, if you notice:

- Vomiting that's severe, frequent, or doesn't improve after a few days
- Inability to keep fluids down at all
- Signs of dehydration — unusual thirst, dark urine, dizziness, or reduced urination
- Vomiting accompanied by severe or persistent abdominal pain (see [Orforglipron and Pancreatitis](/blog/orforglipron-and-pancreatitis/) and [Orforglipron and Gallbladder](/blog/orforglipron-and-gallbladder/) for why that combination is worth evaluating promptly)
- Any GI symptom severe enough that you're considering stopping the medication on your own

None of that is a symptom list to self-diagnose against — it's a prompt to call your prescriber, who can evaluate what's going on and whether it's related to orforglipron, dose timing, or something else entirely.

## Bottom line

Vomiting is a recognized, common GI side effect of orforglipron, less frequent than nausea but in the same labeled category alongside diarrhea and constipation. It tends to be most noticeable during dose increases and is often manageable with smaller, lower-fat meals, steady hydration, and time — but it's not something to self-manage indefinitely if it's severe, persistent, or leaves you unable to keep fluids down. Your prescriber can tell you whether what you're experiencing is typical, whether your titration pace should change, and when it needs more than supportive care.
