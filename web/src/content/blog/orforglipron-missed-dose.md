If you missed a dose of orforglipron (Foundayo), take it as soon as you remember — just don't take an extra tablet the next day to make up for it. That's the core instruction straight from the FDA-approved label. The one exception: if you've missed 7 or more doses in a row, the label says to restart at a lower dose rather than picking back up where you left off.

## What to do if you miss a single dose

Per the FDA-approved FOUNDAYO label on DailyMed, orforglipron's missed-dose instruction is simple:

1. **Take it as soon as possible** once you remember — orforglipron has no food or water timing restriction, so there's no "wrong time of day" to take a late dose the way there might be with a strictly-timed medication.
2. **Do not double up.** The label specifically instructs against taking an extra tablet the next day to compensate. Stick to one tablet per day, even right after a missed one.
3. **Resume your normal schedule** from there — one tablet daily, at whatever time works for you going forward.

This is a much simpler rule than the food/water windows attached to some other GLP-1 pills, which tracks with orforglipron's broader "no restrictions" design covered in our [guide to how to take orforglipron](/blog/how-to-take-orforglipron/).

## What if you miss several days — or a full week?

The label draws a specific line at **7 or more consecutive missed doses**. If you've gone a week or longer without taking orforglipron, the FDA label instructs restarting dosage escalation at a lower dose, rather than simply resuming at whatever level you were on before the gap. The label's rationale is tolerability: a week or more off the medication can mean your tolerance has changed, and jumping straight back into a higher maintenance dose is thought to raise the risk of gastrointestinal side effects like nausea and vomiting.

In practice, that means a missed week isn't something to just push through on your own — it's a signal to check in with your prescriber before restarting, since they can tell you exactly which dose to reinitiate at based on how long you were off and where you were in your titration. ## How orforglipron's rule compares to other GLP-1s

Missed-dose instructions aren't universal across GLP-1 medications — they depend on how often and how the drug is taken. Here's how orforglipron's rule stacks up against semaglutide's injectable and oral forms, per each drug's own labeling:

| | Orforglipron (Foundayo) | Ozempic (semaglutide injection) | Rybelsus (semaglutide oral) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dosing frequency | Once daily | Once weekly | Once daily |
| If you miss a dose | Take it as soon as possible; don't double up the next dose | Per MedlinePlus, can typically still be taken within about 5 days of the missed dose; after that, skip it and resume your regular weekly day | Skip the missed dose and take the next one at your regular time; don't double up |
| Longer gap | 7+ consecutive missed doses → restart titration at a lower dose per the FDA label | Missing doses for an extended stretch (per MedlinePlus, roughly 2 weeks or more) → contact your prescriber about restarting | Extended gaps warrant checking with your prescriber before resuming |

The common thread across all three: never double a dose to "catch up." Where they differ is in the details — a weekly injection has a multi-day catch-up window that a daily pill doesn't need, and orforglipron's food-free dosing means there's no empty-stomach timing to reset the way there is with Rybelsus. If you're comparing the two oral options directly, [orforglipron vs. Rybelsus](/blog/orforglipron-vs-rybelsus/) covers the broader differences beyond missed doses, and [orforglipron vs. Ozempic](/blog/orforglipron-vs-ozempic/) covers the pill-vs-injection comparison in full.

## Why the "don't double up" rule matters

Doubling a GLP-1 dose isn't just against the instructions — GLP-1 receptor agonists as a class are generally associated with more gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) at higher doses, which is part of why orforglipron is titrated upward slowly in the first place rather than starting at a high dose. Taking two tablets in a day effectively jumps you to double your current dose level for that day, working against the gradual approach the titration schedule is built around. ## Building a habit that avoids the problem

Because orforglipron has no food or water rules to anchor to, some people find it easier to forget to take orforglipron, or lose track of whether they've taken it, than with a medication tied to a specific ritual. The most reliable fix isn't remembering harder — it's removing the need to remember at all: pick one existing daily habit (brushing your teeth, making coffee, a phone alarm) and always take the tablet at that exact moment, every day, without exception. Keeping the bottle somewhere visible rather than in a cabinet also removes the "did I already take it?" uncertainty that leads to accidental double doses or accidental skips alike.

## When to involve your prescriber

Reach out to your prescriber if:

- You've missed **7 or more consecutive doses** and need guidance on restarting.
- You accidentally took two doses in one day, especially if you're having significant side effects.
- Side effects return or intensify after restarting following a gap.
- You're unsure how a specific missed-dose situation applies to your titration step.

This article explains the general label instructions, not a substitute for guidance tailored to your specific dose, health history, or how long you've been off the medication — your prescriber or pharmacist is the right resource for that.
