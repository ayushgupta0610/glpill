If you've taken more orforglipron (Foundayo) than intended, the FDA-approved label doesn't describe a specific overdose syndrome — its guidance is general: contact Poison Control or a medical toxicologist, and get supportive treatment based on whatever symptoms actually show up. Because the drug clears slowly, a period of observation may be needed rather than a quick resolution. Here's what the label says, what symptoms to watch for, and when to seek care.

## What the FDA label says about overdose

Per the FOUNDAYO label on DailyMed, the overdosage guidance is straightforward and general, not orforglipron-specific:

- **Contact Poison Control** (1-800-222-1222) **or a medical toxicologist** for current recommendations.
- **Initiate supportive treatment** according to the person's actual clinical signs and symptoms — there isn't a single standard "overdose protocol" beyond treating what appears.
- **A period of observation and treatment may be necessary**, considering orforglipron's half-life. Per the FDA label, that half-life is approximately 29 to 49 hours (see our [half-life guide](/blog/how-long-does-orforglipron-stay-in-your-system/) for the full clearance timeline), which is longer than many oral medications — so symptoms aren't necessarily a same-day issue.

This is a common pattern for GLP-1 receptor agonist labeling generally: there's no specific antidote, and management centers on supportive care rather than a targeted reversal treatment.

## What symptoms could an overdose cause?

The label doesn't list a distinct set of overdose-specific symptoms, but it's reasonable to expect an exaggerated version of orforglipron's known dose-related effects:

| Symptom category | What to watch for |
|---|---|
| Gastrointestinal | Worsened or severe nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, or abdominal discomfort |
| Dehydration (from GI effects) | Dark or infrequent urination, dizziness, unusual thirst, fatigue |
| Low blood sugar (mainly if also on insulin or a sulfonylurea) | Shakiness, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat, lightheadedness |

GLP-1 receptor agonists as a class are most strongly associated with gastrointestinal side effects, and those effects tend to be dose-related — meaning more of the drug at once plausibly means more pronounced GI symptoms, even though the label doesn't quantify this for an overdose scenario specifically. If you're already managing nausea or vomiting at a normal dose, [Managing Orforglipron Nausea](/blog/managing-orforglipron-nausea/) and [Orforglipron Vomiting](/blog/orforglipron-vomiting/) cover the day-to-day version of these symptoms; an overdose is the same symptom categories, potentially more intense.

## Accidental double dose vs. a true overdose

Not every "I took too much" situation is the same. Two common scenarios:

- **Accidentally took two tablets in one day** (for example, forgetting you already took it, or trying to "catch up" after a missed dose). The label's own missed-dose instructions explicitly warn against doubling up for this reason. If this happens, don't panic, but do call your prescriber or Poison Control to talk through your specific situation rather than assuming it's automatically fine or automatically dangerous. Our [missed dose guide](/blog/orforglipron-missed-dose/) covers the correct way to handle a missed dose so this doesn't happen in the first place.
- **Took a larger quantity than a single extra tablet**, whether by mistake or otherwise. This warrants contacting Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) promptly, and emergency care if symptoms are significant.

Either way, the label's core instruction applies: get guidance based on your actual symptoms rather than guessing at home.

## Dehydration and kidney strain risk

Because severe vomiting or diarrhea can lead to dehydration, and dehydration can in turn strain kidney function, an overdose scenario with pronounced GI symptoms is one where that chain is more likely to matter. Our [Orforglipron and Kidney Function](/blog/orforglipron-and-kidney-function/) guide covers the dehydration warning signs worth watching for — dark urine, dizziness, unusual thirst — in more detail. If those appear after taking too much orforglipron, that's a reason to seek care rather than wait it out.

## Does an overdose raise hypoglycemia risk?

Mostly in combination with other glucose-lowering medications. GLP-1 receptor agonists engage a glucose-dependent insulin pathway, which is part of why hypoglycemia isn't the headline concern with these drugs alone. But if you also take insulin or an insulin secretagogue like a sulfonylurea, taking more orforglipron than intended could plausibly compound that risk. If you're on any glucose-lowering medication and think you've taken too much orforglipron, mention that combination specifically when you call Poison Control or your prescriber.

## How this compares across GLP-1 medications

Overdose management for GLP-1 receptor agonists follows a similar general pattern across the class, per MedlinePlus drug information for semaglutide and tirzepatide:

| | Orforglipron (Foundayo) | Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus) | Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific antidote | None described | None described, per MedlinePlus | None described, per MedlinePlus |
| General approach | Contact Poison Control; supportive treatment based on symptoms | Contact Poison Control; supportive treatment, per MedlinePlus | Contact Poison Control; supportive treatment, per MedlinePlus |
| Why observation matters | Half-life of ~29–49 hours means effects don't clear quickly | Longer-acting formulations mean effects can persist, per MedlinePlus | Longer-acting formulations mean effects can persist, per MedlinePlus |

The pattern holds across the class: no targeted reversal treatment, and management built around watching for and treating whatever symptoms actually develop.

## What to do right now if you've taken too much

1. **Call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222** (US) for immediate, situation-specific guidance, or contact a medical toxicologist.
2. **Call your prescriber** to let them know what happened, especially if you take other medications like insulin or a sulfonylurea.
3. **Go to the emergency room** if you have severe or persistent vomiting, can't keep fluids down, show signs of significant dehydration, have symptoms of low blood sugar that don't resolve, or feel like something is seriously wrong.
4. **Don't take your next scheduled dose early** to "balance it out" — resume your normal one-tablet-daily schedule once you've been advised it's safe to do so.

## Bottom line

There's no orforglipron-specific overdose syndrome described in the FDA label, and no specific antidote. If you've taken more than intended, contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or your prescriber right away, watch for worsening GI symptoms or, if you're on insulin or a sulfonylurea, low blood sugar, and seek emergency care for anything severe. Because orforglipron's half-life runs 1 to 2 days, don't assume symptoms will resolve within hours — a period of observation is part of the label's own guidance.
