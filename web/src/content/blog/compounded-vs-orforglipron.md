Compounded orforglipron, semaglutide, and tirzepatide are custom-prepared by compounding pharmacies and are not themselves FDA-approved drug products, even when they contain the same active ingredient as an approved medication. Orforglipron, sold under the brand Foundayo, is FDA-approved — it went through the FDA's full review of safety, effectiveness, and manufacturing quality. Compounded orforglipron also exists, and it carries the same distinction: same active ingredient, different regulatory status. Here's what that difference actually means, without guessing at numbers no one has published.

## What "compounded" means for a GLP-1 medication

A compounded drug is prepared by a pharmacy for an individual patient, typically starting from bulk active pharmaceutical ingredient, rather than being manufactured and sold as a finished, FDA-approved product. Compounding itself is legal and has a long history in medicine — it fills real gaps, like when a patient needs a dose strength or formulation that isn't commercially available. Compounded weight loss drugs became a familiar term in the GLP-1 space specifically because of injectable shortages, not because compounding is unique to this drug class.

Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide became widespread while the FDA-approved injectable versions (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound) were in shortage, since federal law allows compounding of a drug that's officially listed as unavailable. Some compounding pharmacies now also offer compounded orforglipron. What compounding does *not* do is put the product through the FDA's premarket review — the agency doesn't evaluate or approve individual compounded formulations the way it approves a brand-name drug like Foundayo.

## Is Foundayo FDA-approved or compounded?

Per its DailyMed label, Foundayo is an FDA-approved once-daily oral GLP-1 receptor agonist tablet, taken with or without food and no water restriction, approved for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with at least one weight-related condition. Every batch is manufactured to FDA-regulated quality standards, and the dose, formulation, and manufacturing process are fixed by the approval — you get the same product every time you fill the prescription.

That consistency is the core practical difference from a compounded product: a compounded formulation is prepared pharmacy-by-pharmacy, and its exact composition, purity, and dosing accuracy aren't verified against the FDA's approval standard.

## Why does the compounded-vs-approved distinction matter for safety?

The active ingredients in GLP-1 medications — orforglipron, semaglutide, tirzepatide — have documented safety and side-effect profiles because they went through FDA-reviewed clinical trials in their approved forms. Per MedlinePlus and the Foundayo label, common side effects across this drug class are gastrointestinal: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and constipation, especially during dose increases.

A compounded product isn't guaranteed to match that same safety picture. Some compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide formulations have used salt forms of the active ingredient, or other formulation choices, that differ from what was studied in the trials supporting Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound — meaning the safety and effectiveness data for the approved drug doesn't automatically transfer to every compounded version. Dosing accuracy is also a real-world concern with compounded products dispensed in multidose vials, since it shifts more of the measurement burden onto the patient compared with a pre-set tablet or pen.
