An orforglipron weight loss plateau — a stretch where the scale stops moving even though you're still taking your daily dose — is a common concern, and it doesn't automatically mean the medication has stopped working. It can reflect where you are in the titration schedule, normal week-to-week fluctuation, or other factors worth discussing with your prescriber. This article explains what's actually known and what isn't, without guessing at numbers or timelines that aren't yours to guess at.

## What counts as a plateau?

There's no single, medically defined cutoff for when a stall in weight loss becomes a "plateau" worth acting on — this article won't invent one. In general use, people describe a plateau as a period of weeks where weight stays roughly flat despite continuing the medication and their usual habits. Whether a particular stretch of flat weeks is meaningful for you, or just normal fluctuation, is something your prescriber is better positioned to assess than a generic article.

## Why does an orforglipron weight loss plateau happen?

A few commonly discussed factors can contribute to a weight-loss slowdown on any GLP-1 medication, orforglipron included:

- **You may still be titrating.** Per the FDA-approved label, orforglipron starts at 0.8 mg once daily and increases roughly every 30 days — to 2.5 mg, then 5.5 mg, and optionally further to 9 mg, 14.5 mg, and a maximum of 17.2 mg, based on response and tolerability. Effects can vary by dose step, so a stall early in titration is a different situation than one at a stable maintenance dose. Our [Foundayo (orforglipron) guide](/blog/foundayo-orforglipron-guide/) covers the full schedule.
- **Weight loss rate naturally changes over time.** It's widely discussed, across weight-management approaches generally, that the pace of loss often isn't linear — faster early on, then slower as the body adjusts. That pattern isn't unique to any one drug.
- **Body composition shifts can mask the number on the scale.** Changes in muscle relative to fat can affect what the scale shows even when meaningful changes are still happening.
- **Non-medication factors.** Sleep, stress, activity level, and eating patterns are all commonly cited contributors to a plateau in general weight-management discussions. If your routine has shifted, that's worth mentioning to your prescriber alongside the plateau itself.

## How does the titration step relate to the scale?

| Titration step | Dose (once daily) | What the label says about timing |

| 1 | 0.8 mg | Starting dose |

| 3 | 5.5 mg | After at least 30 days on 2.5 mg |
| 4 (optional) | 9 mg | After at least 30 days on 5.5 mg, based on response/tolerability |
| 5 (optional) | 14.5 mg | After at least 30 days on 9 mg, based on response/tolerability |
| 6 (optional) | 17.2 mg (maximum) | After at least 30 days on 14.5 mg, based on response/tolerability |

This table is the FDA label's titration schedule, not a weight-loss timeline — it doesn't say how much weight loss to expect at any step, and this article won't invent one. What it does show is that each step is held for at least a month, so a flat week or two within a single step isn't unusual before the next scheduled increase (if your prescriber determines one is appropriate).

## Should the dose be increased to break a plateau?

That's a decision for your prescriber, not something to adjust on your own. The label's titration schedule exists specifically so increases happen based on response and tolerability, evaluated by the person prescribing the medication — not on a self-directed timeline tied to the scale. If you're on a stable, non-increasing dose already and think an increase might be appropriate, that's a conversation to have directly, not a change to make independently.

## What's worth bringing to your prescriber

- How many weeks the scale has been flat, and at which titration step.
- Any changes in sleep, stress, activity, or appetite around the same time.
- Whether you've missed doses — [missed-dose guidance](/blog/orforglipron-missed-dose/) covers what the label says, since missed doses can also affect the pattern you're seeing.
- Whether a dose increase is appropriate given your tolerability so far.

Bringing specifics — not just "it stalled" — gives your prescriber something concrete to evaluate. If you've been logging doses and weight, our [tracking your GLP-1 pill routine](/blog/track-glp1-pill-routine/) piece covers a simple way to keep that record.

## Bottom line

A plateau on orforglipron is a common enough experience to be worth understanding, but it isn't something this article can diagnose or resolve for you — there's no universal week count, pound threshold, or dose-increase rule that applies to everyone. What the FDA label does establish is the titration schedule itself: doses increase roughly every 30 days based on response and tolerability, as determined by your prescriber. If your weight loss has stalled, the most useful next step is a specific conversation with your prescriber about your titration step, timeline, and any other changes in your routine — not a generic fix applied on your own.
