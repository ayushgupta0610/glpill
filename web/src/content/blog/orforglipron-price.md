If you're searching "orforglipron price," you probably already know Foundayo is the first daily GLP-1 pill approved for weight management — and now you want to know what it actually costs. The honest answer: it depends almost entirely on how you're paying. Here's what Eli Lilly has published, what shapes the number you'll actually see, and where to check for the current price before you plan around it.

## This is educational, not financial or medical advice

Nothing here is a guarantee of what you personally will pay, and it isn't medical advice about whether Foundayo is right for you. Drug pricing changes — sometimes month to month — and your actual cost depends on your dose, insurance plan, pharmacy, and any savings programs you qualify for. Always confirm the current price with Lilly's official site, your pharmacy, or your insurer.

## What Eli Lilly has published

Per Eli Lilly's own announcement and its LillyDirect Foundayo page, three price points show up depending on how you pay:

- **Self-pay (no insurance), through LillyDirect:** starting at **$149/month** for the 0.8 mg starting dose, stepping up with the dose — roughly **$199/month** at 2.5 mg and **$299/month** at 5.5 mg and the higher maintenance doses.
- **Commercial insurance with the Foundayo savings card:** eligible, commercially insured patients with coverage for Foundayo may pay **as little as $25/month**.
- **Medicare Part D:** eligible patients may be able to get Foundayo for **around $50/month**.

These are Lilly's own published figures as of this writing — not a universal price. Terms and conditions apply to the savings card and Medicare offer (for example, government-insured patients typically aren't eligible for the commercial savings card), and Lilly has not published a separate standalone list price (WAC) the way some pricing databases show for other drugs. What you'll encounter as a patient is one of the numbers above, not a separate "sticker price."

> **The one thing to remember:** the price is not a single number. It's a range that depends on whether you're self-pay, insured, or on Medicare — and it changes as you move up the dose titration.

## Why the price moves with your dose

Foundayo is started low and titrated upward over months (see our [Foundayo dosing guide](/blog/foundayo-orforglipron-guide/) for the full schedule). Lilly's self-pay pricing tracks that: the 0.8 mg starting dose is priced lower than the 2.5 mg step, which is priced lower than the 5.5 mg–17.2 mg maintenance range. In practice, that means your monthly self-pay cost in month one is likely to be lower than your cost once you reach a maintenance dose several months in — worth factoring into a monthly budget rather than assuming a flat number for the whole year.

## Insurance and coverage

Whether insurance covers Foundayo — and how much you'll owe — depends on your specific plan. Some commercial plans cover GLP-1s for weight management, often requiring prior authorization; others don't cover weight-management indications at all, or cover them with a higher copay than diabetes indications. Medicare Part D coverage for weight-management GLP-1s has also been evolving.

Because this varies so much by plan, the only reliable way to know your actual cost is to:

1. Ask your insurer directly whether Foundayo is covered for your indication, and what your copay or coinsurance would be.
2. Ask your pharmacy to run a test claim so you see the real out-of-pocket number before you commit.
3. Check whether you're eligible for Lilly's savings card, which can significantly lower the cost for commercially insured patients.

## Savings cards and self-pay programs

Manufacturer savings programs are often the biggest lever on price. Lilly's Foundayo savings card is aimed at commercially insured patients with coverage for the drug, and its LillyDirect self-pay path is aimed at people without insurance coverage for it — these are different programs with different eligibility, so don't assume one applies if you qualify for the other. Programs like this also tend to have terms that can change (income limits, government-insurance exclusions, refill-timing requirements for promotional pricing), so read the current terms rather than relying on a secondhand summary — including this one.

## Cash pay vs. going through insurance

If you don't have coverage for Foundayo, cash/self-pay through LillyDirect is generally going to be cheaper than paying full price at a retail pharmacy without any assistance, since it's Lilly's own direct-to-patient pricing. If you do have insurance, it's worth comparing your insured copay against the self-pay price — occasionally the self-pay or savings-card price is actually lower than a high-deductible plan's copay, especially early in the plan year.

## Cost vs. injectable GLP-1s

Orforglipron is a **small-molecule pill**, not an injectable peptide like semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) or tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro). That distinction gets discussed a lot in coverage of the drug because small molecules are generally simpler and cheaper to manufacture and scale than injectable peptides — which is part of the reason some analysts and outlets have speculated oral GLP-1s could eventually put downward pressure on GLP-1 pricing broadly.

That's useful context, but it's not a promise of a specific discount. Injectable GLP-1 pricing also varies widely by self-pay program, insurance, and dose, and both drug classes are still relatively new to the market with pricing that continues to shift. If cost is a deciding factor between an oral and injectable option, compare current self-pay and insured prices for each directly rather than assuming pills are automatically cheaper.
