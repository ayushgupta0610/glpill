Orforglipron (Foundayo) does not need refrigeration. Per its FDA-approved label on DailyMed, it's stored at room temperature — 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with brief excursions to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) allowed — and it's light-sensitive, so it stays in its original bottle. That single fact is why storage and travel look completely different for orforglipron than for injectable GLP-1s.

## Does orforglipron need to be refrigerated?

No. Unlike injectable GLP-1 pens, which typically require refrigeration before their first use, FOUNDAYO tablets are shelf-stable at normal room temperature from the moment you pick up the bottle. There's no cold-chain window to manage, no "first use" clock that changes the storage rules, and no risk of a pen freezing in the back of a fridge and having to be discarded. You store it the way you'd store most oral medications: at room temperature, in its original container.

## How to store Foundayo: exact temperature conditions

In practice, orforglipron room temperature storage is simple — no fridge, no ice packs, nothing to schedule around. Per the FDA label, orforglipron should be kept at:

- **Room temperature:** 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F)
- **Permitted excursions:** 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) — for brief periods, such as during a normal trip or a short stretch in a warm room
- **Protected from light:** kept in the original bottle and carton, with the cap replaced after each use

That light-sensitivity detail is easy to miss. It means the safest habit is leaving tablets in the bottle they came in — not transferring a week's supply into a clear pill organizer that sits on a sunny windowsill or counter.

## Can you travel with orforglipron?

The absence of a cold chain is the practical headline for travel. You're not packing a cooler bag, tracking ice-pack timing, or worrying about a pen thawing out in checked luggage. A few general habits still apply, the same as for any prescription medication:

- **Keep tablets in the original, labeled bottle.** This matters twice over for orforglipron, since the bottle also protects against light — and it makes the medication easy to identify if you're asked about it while traveling.
- **Pack it in your carry-on, not checked luggage.** Cargo holds can swing well outside the label's permitted temperature range, and checked bags can be delayed or lost.
- **Don't leave the bottle in a hot car or direct sun for extended periods**, even though brief excursions up to 30°C (86°F) are within spec. A car interior on a summer day routinely exceeds that.
- **Check current airline and security guidance before you fly.** Rules and screening procedures can change, and this article is a storage guide, not a travel-regulations source — confirm specifics with your airline or the relevant security agency directly.

## Everyday storage at home

Day to day, the practical habits are simple:

- **Leave tablets in the original bottle**, cap replaced after each use, rather than moving a week's supply into an open pill organizer — that protects against both light exposure and the moisture that can degrade tablets over time.
- **Skip the bathroom medicine cabinet if you can.** It's a common storage spot, but the humidity from regular showers isn't ideal for moisture-sensitive oral tablets. A kitchen cabinet, closet shelf, or bedroom drawer — somewhere dry and out of direct light — is a better fit for the label's storage conditions.
- **Keep it out of reach of children and pets**, same as any prescription medication, regardless of how it's stored.

None of this requires special equipment — no cooler, no ice packs, no refrigerator shelf space — which is a meaningful, if unglamorous, quality-of-life difference from managing an injectable GLP-1's cold chain.

## What if a bottle was exposed to heat or cold outside the permitted range?

The label's excursion range (15°C–30°C / 59°F–86°F) covers brief, normal fluctuations — not a bottle baking in a car for a full afternoon or freezing in checked luggage in winter. If you're not sure whether a specific bottle is still good after an extended temperature exposure, don't guess either direction. Call your pharmacist, describe what happened, and ask whether that batch is still safe to use — replacing a compromised bottle is a minor inconvenience next to taking a tablet that may have degraded.

## Bottom line

Orforglipron's room-temperature, no-refrigeration storage is one of the quieter advantages of it being a small-molecule pill rather than an injectable peptide. Keep it in its original, light-protective bottle, stay within the label's 15–30°C excursion range, and travel with it in your carry-on rather than checked luggage.
