Yes, orforglipron (Foundayo) can cause heartburn — gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), dyspepsia, and belching (eructation) are listed among the gastrointestinal adverse reactions on the FDA-approved label, in the same group as nausea, vomiting, constipation, and diarrhea. It tends to cluster around dose increases and is usually manageable with simple supportive steps, but reflux that's severe or paired with chest pain or trouble swallowing deserves a call to your prescriber, not a home remedy.

## Why does orforglipron cause heartburn?

Orforglipron is a **GLP-1 receptor agonist** — it works by mimicking a gut hormone that slows gastric emptying and affects appetite signaling. When the stomach empties more slowly, food and acid sit inside it longer than usual, which can increase stomach volume and pressure. That extra pressure pushes against the lower esophageal sphincter, the muscle that's supposed to keep stomach contents from traveling back up — and when it gives, acid escapes into the esophagus and causes the burning sensation of heartburn, along with belching or regurgitation. It's the same slowed-digestion mechanism that supports appetite reduction and fullness, which is part of why reflux-type symptoms show up across the GLP-1 class generally, not just with orforglipron. For the fuller mechanism, see [How Does Orforglipron Work?](/blog/how-does-orforglipron-work/)

## How common is heartburn with orforglipron?

Per the FOUNDAYO label on DailyMed, gastroesophageal reflux disease, dyspepsia, and eructation are among the gastrointestinal adverse reactions reported in orforglipron's clinical trials, grouped alongside nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, abdominal pain, and abdominal distension as effects reported in a meaningful share of patients. The label doesn't treat reflux symptoms as rare — they're part of the expected GI side-effect pattern for this drug class, which is also documented for semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) on MedlinePlus. Individual experience varies widely: some people never notice it, others get a stretch of it during a dose step-up, and a smaller group finds it persistent enough to bring up with their prescriber.

## Heartburn vs. the other GI side effects — how it fits together

Heartburn rarely shows up in isolation. It's part of a cluster of digestion-related effects that share the same root cause: slower gastric emptying.

| GI effect | How it relates to heartburn |
|---|---|
| Dyspepsia / indigestion | A related "upper stomach" discomfort, often reported together with heartburn |
| Belching (eructation) | Often accompanies reflux as trapped gas and acid move upward |
| Bloating | Shares the same gastric-emptying mechanism; see [Orforglipron Bloating](/blog/orforglipron-bloating/) |
| Nausea | Also tied to slower stomach emptying; see [Managing Orforglipron Nausea](/blog/managing-orforglipron-nausea/) |
| Constipation | Slower transit can also mean slower stool movement; see [Orforglipron and Constipation](/blog/orforglipron-constipation/) |

There's no rule about which of these you'll get, if any — the label lists all of them as possible, not guaranteed, effects.

## Does this happen with other GLP-1 medications too?

Yes — reflux-type symptoms aren't unique to orforglipron. Per MedlinePlus drug information, gastrointestinal effects are a recognized part of the profile for semaglutide, whether injected (Ozempic, Wegovy) or taken orally (Rybelsus), and for tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) as well. All of these medications work by slowing gastric emptying, so the same acid-and-pressure mechanism behind orforglipron heartburn applies across the class. That doesn't mean the rate or severity is identical between drugs — there's no published head-to-head trial comparing reflux specifically across orforglipron, semaglutide, and tirzepatide — but if you've switched between GLP-1 medications and heartburn showed up or changed, that's consistent with how this drug class generally behaves, not a sign something unusual is happening.

## What helps manage heartburn on orforglipron

These are general, commonly discussed supportive habits for GLP-1-related GI side effects — not medical advice, and not a substitute for calling your prescriber if heartburn is frequent, severe, or paired with pain:

- **Eat smaller meals, more slowly.** A large meal in a stomach that's already emptying slowly adds more volume and pressure, which can push acid upward.
- **Stay upright after eating.** Avoid lying down for a couple of hours after a meal, and consider propping up the head of your bed if nighttime reflux is an issue.
- **Go easy on known reflux triggers** — caffeine, alcohol, carbonated drinks, and fatty, fried, or spicy food can all make heartburn more likely. If caffeine specifically is part of your routine, [Orforglipron and Caffeine](/blog/orforglipron-and-caffeine/) has more on how the two interact, and [Orforglipron and Alcohol](/blog/orforglipron-and-alcohol/) covers the same for drinking.
- **Don't self-adjust your dose.** If heartburn tracks closely with a recent dose increase, your prescriber — not a self-directed skip or reduction — is the right person to decide whether to slow the titration pace.
- **Check before adding an antacid or reflux medication.** Over-the-counter options exist, but your prescriber or pharmacist should weigh in first so it doesn't interact with anything else you're taking.

If food choices in general are the harder piece of the puzzle, [Eating on Orforglipron](/blog/eating-on-orforglipron/) goes deeper into meal patterns that tend to sit more comfortably during titration.

## When to contact your prescriber

Reach out rather than waiting it out if you notice:

- Heartburn that's severe, constant, or doesn't ease after several weeks at a stable dose
- Reflux accompanied by chest pain, trouble swallowing, or vomiting
- Any GI symptom severe enough that you're considering stopping the medication on your own

None of that is a checklist to self-diagnose against — it's a prompt to call your prescriber, who can evaluate whether what you're experiencing is typical GLP-1 reflux, tied to dose timing, or something that needs a closer look.

## Bottom line

Heartburn, acid reflux, and related upper-GI discomfort are recognized side effects of orforglipron, grouped with nausea, bloating, constipation, and dyspepsia on the FDA-approved FOUNDAYO label. It's driven by the same slowed-digestion mechanism that makes the drug work, tends to be most noticeable around dose increases, and is often manageable with smaller meals, staying upright after eating, and cutting back on common reflux triggers. Severe, persistent, or painful reflux isn't something to self-treat indefinitely — your prescriber can tell you whether your titration pace should change and when a symptom needs more than supportive care.
